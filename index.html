The first letter of Scripture and the last together spell the Son. He is the beginning and the end.
A lesser-known devotional · the scarlet worm
“But I am a worm, and no man”Psalm 22:6
תּוֹלַעַת
tola’ath
the crimson dye-worm of the oak · Kermes vermilio
As tradition has long told it:
She fastens herself to the wood and cannot come away without dying.
Her young shelter beneath her, and for some three days they feed on her body.
Dying, her crimson stains the wood and her children, marking them for life.
Then her body pales, dry as wax, and flakes away white as wool.
David took the lowliest word for himself, not a man but a worm. An old devotion reads the cross in it: the One fixed to the wood, his body given for his own, his blood their covering, the scarlet made white as snow.
Prophecy & fulfilment · the colt foretold
“Behold, thy King will come to thee... riding upon an ass, and upon a colt”Zechariah 9:9
One thread, told across a thousand years:
Jacob blesses Judah, binding his foal to the vine, the sceptre that would not depart. Genesis 49
Zechariah sees the King come meek, upon a colt, the foal of an ass. Zechariah 9
On Palm Sunday He rides that colt into Jerusalem, “that it might be fulfilled.” Matthew 21 · John 12
And devotion sees the cross it bore marked ever since upon its back.
From Judah’s foal, to Zechariah’s colt, to the gates of the city: the King came lowly, and the beast that carried Him wears the cross still.
Prophecy & fulfilment · the house of bread
“And thou, Bethlehem... out of thee shall come forth the ruler of Israel”Micah 5:2
בֵּית לֶחֶם
Beth-lechem
house of bread
One thread, from the manger to the table:
Micah named the town: from Bethlehem comes the ruler of Israel. Micah 5:2 · Matthew 2:6
Its name, Beth-lechem, means “house of bread.”
There the Child was laid in a manger (φατνη), the trough where animals feed. Luke 2:7
At the table He took bread: “This is my body, given for you.” Luke 22:19
And devotion sees one Bread: born in the house of bread, laid where creatures feed, given as food at the table.
Born in the house of bread, laid where the beasts feed, given as bread at the table: He called Himself the living bread come down from heaven.
A Latin devotional · Eva reversed
“The mother of all the living”Genesis 3:20
EVA
Eve, in the Latin
AVE
hail, full of grace
A reversal the Latin tongue heard:
Eve, Hebrew Chavvah, the mother of all the living, through whom came the fall. Genesis 3:20
To Mary the angel’s word is χαιρε (chaire) in the Greek; the Latin dresses it as Ave. Luke 1:28
And in Latin, Ave is Eva turned around: the greeting reverses the name.
Long before, Irenaeus said Mary’s obedience untied the knot Eve’s disobedience had bound: the new Eve.
What was bound in Eva, the Latin ear heard loosed in Ave: her “yes” reversing the first “no.”
The Two Goats · bar-abba
Bar-abba is Aramaic for “son of the father.” At the trial the crowd is handed a choice between two men.
Jesus Barabbas
son of the father
set free
Jesus the Christ
the Son of the Father
condemned
The name is bar-abba: bar, son; abba, father. Aramaic
Part of the tradition names him “Jesus Barabbas,” so two men called Jesus stood before the crowd; Origen knew the reading and the text critics judge it original, the name later dropped for reverence. Matthew 27:16–17
The Day of Atonement set two goats by lot: one slain for the LORD, one released for Azazel, bearing the people’s sin away. Leviticus 16
The reading · what the Fathers heard
From Barnabas and Justin to Origen, the two goats were read together as Christ: the one slain, and the one that carries sin away.
At the trial the lot falls the same way: the guilty son of the father walks free because the innocent Son of the Father is condemned in his place.
The wrong man went free, and that is the gospel in miniature: the guilty released, the innocent bound, the true Son standing in the place of the son of the father.
The Veil
On the second day God set the firmament between the waters above and below. Of all six days of work, this alone he never calls good, and the same barrier runs all the way to the cross.
Five times Genesis records “and God saw that it was good”; a sixth time, “very good.” Day two alone passes in silence. Genesis 1:6–8, 31
When man is driven from the garden, cherubim and a flaming sword are set to guard the way back to the tree of life. Genesis 3:24
In the tabernacle the same cherubim are woven into the veil itself, and only the high priest may pass beyond it, once a year. Exodus 26:31 · Leviticus 16
Solomon weaves cherubim into the Temple veil too; the barrier becomes permanent, set in cedar and gold rather than goat-hair tent. 2 Chronicles 3:14
fulfilled
At the moment Christ breathed his last, that same veil was torn, not from the bottom by human hands reaching up, but from top to bottom, by the only hand that could reach down. Matthew 27:51
What day two withheld is given at Calvary: the division finishes its purpose by being undone.
The Fig Leaves
Adam and Eve sewed fig leaves and covered themselves the moment their eyes were opened. At the cross, Christ is uncovered by others’ hands, and does not resist it.
Their eyes were opened and they knew they were naked; they sewed fig leaves and made themselves coverings. Genesis 3:7
It is the first human act after the fall, not repentance, but self-covering, done before God has even spoken a word.
Centuries later, soldiers at the cross strip Christ of his garments and divide them among themselves, casting lots. Matthew 27:35 · John 19:23–24
fulfilled
Adam covers his own shame by his own hand; the new Adam is uncovered by others’ hands and does not resist it, like a lamb led silently before its shearers. Isaiah 53:7
The first covering was sewn in fear. The last uncovering is borne in silence, and nothing is hidden anymore.
Wood on His Back
Abraham placed the wood on Isaac’s back and led him up the hill. Two thousand years later, the Son went out carrying his own cross up another hill, at his Father’s direction.
Abraham took the wood for the burnt offering and laid it on Isaac his son; and he took the fire in his hand and the knife. Genesis 22:6
Isaac said: “Here is the fire and the wood, but where is the lamb for the burnt offering?” Abraham replied: “God himself will provide the lamb.” Genesis 22:7–8
So they went both of them together. Genesis 22:8
Jesus went out, carrying his own cross, to the place called the Place of a Skull, which in Aramaic is called Golgotha. John 19:17
patristic
Justin Martyr is the first to name it plainly: the wood placed on Isaac’s back was a figure of the cross Christ would carry. The same observation recurs in Irenaeus, Tertullian, and Origen. Justin, Dial. 111
Both sons carry the wood of their own sacrifice up a hill, willingly, at their father’s word. In both cases the father provides what the son could not provide for himself.
Among the Trees
Adam and Eve hid themselves among the trees of the garden, away from their Maker. Christ does not hide. He is lifted up on a tree, in full view.
“I heard your voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself.” Genesis 3:10
From among the trees of the garden they had eaten; among the same trees they now hide.
“Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree,” the law had said; Paul names the cross with that very word. Galatians 3:13 · Deuteronomy 21:23
fulfilled
Where Adam withdrew into hiding, the new Adam is lifted up in the open, on a tree, for all to see; the apostles preach the cross by that same name from their very first sermons. Acts 5:30 · 1 Peter 2:24
The first man hid among the trees, afraid. The new Adam hangs on a tree, in the open, that nothing further need be hidden.
